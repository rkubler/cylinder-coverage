Cylinder Shot Peening Coverage Simulator

A cylinder of radius R turns at N rpm while the nozzle, aimed at its axis, travels along it at the traverse speed V. Relative to the surface, the jet draws a helix: one turn of the cylinder moves the nozzle by the pitch p = 60·V/N. When the pitch is large compared with the jet size ΔX, the impacts pile up in helical stripes. The page builds the map of impacts per point λ on the whole surface, then the coverage τ = 100·(1 − e−λ), and compares it with the average model.

Parameters

Defaults: media and jet of the reference spreadsheet model (ṁ = 1.5 kg/min, ρ = 2.2 g/cm³, D = 150 µm, r = 10 µm, ΔX = 20 mm, Gaussian jet) on a cylinder of radius 25 mm and length 150 mm turning at 24 rpm, with a traverse speed of 8 mm/s: the pitch is 20 mm, as large as the jet, so the helix is clearly visible. Two passes back and forth. Changing a parameter restarts the simulation.

How the cylinder model works

  1. E[D³]: mean of the cube of the shot diameterThe mass of a shot is proportional to D³, so the mean shot mass needs E[D³], the mean of D³ over all shots. Identical shots: E[D³] = D³. Log-normal sizes with mass median D₅₀: E[D³] = D₅₀³·e^(−4.5σ²).
  2. Shots per secondṄ = ṁ / m̄ₚ, with m̄ₚ = ρ·π/6·E[D³]
  3. Area of one denta = π·c²·E[D²]·f(γ), c = r₅₀/D₅₀, E[D²] = D₅₀²·e^(−4σ²); angle of incidence at the aim point cos γ = cos θ·cos ψ, f = cos γ or cos²γ . The jet is ΔX·√(1 + tan²θ/cos²ψ) long along the axis, which smooths the helical stripes; ψ makes the jet miss the axis (cross-section view).
  4. Surface speed and helixVs = 2πR·N/60. Relative to the surface the nozzle moves at (V, Vs): a helix of pitch p = V·60/N = 2πR·V/Vs and angle α = atan(V/Vs).
  5. Shots that hit the cylinderThe jet is a parallel stream of width ΔX aimed at the axis. A shot at a distance u from the axis plane hits the surface at the arc s = R·asin(u/R); shots with |u| > R miss. Density per unit arc: gs(s) = g(R·sin(s/R))·cos(s/R). Fraction that hits: c = G(R) − G(−R).
  6. Average K of one passKc = c·a·Ṅ / (2πR·V): one pass spreads the shots of L/V seconds over the area 2πR·L. Equivalent to a flat raster with step p and line speed Vs: Kc = c·a·Ṅ/(Vs·p). It does not depend on the rotation speed.
  7. Local impacts per pointAlong the helical coordinate ζ = z − s·p/(2πR) (forward pass) or ζ = z + s·p/(2πR) (return pass), λ is periodic with period p: λ(ζ) = a·Ṅ·Σk h(ζ − k·p), with the track profile h(ζ) = (1/Vs)·∫ g(ζ − σ·tan α)·gs(σ) dσ.
  8. Stripe amplitude, one pass(λ max − λ min) / λ mean along ζ. Gaussian jet: ≈ 4·e^(−2π²(σj/p)²), below 3 % for p ≤ ΔX/2.
  9. Rotation speed without stripesFor a Gaussian jet, p ≤ ΔX/2 gives N ≥ 120·V/ΔX (rpm).
  10. Coverage above 100 % (NF L 06-832)Base time tb: the time to dent 98 % of the surface (here from the helical stripe model). Full coverage, 100 %, is 1.25·tb (an option sets it to tb instead); a coverage C means C/100 times the time of full coverage (200 % = twice that time). On an even treatment, 1.25·tb gives λ₁₀₀ = 1.25·ln 50 = 4.89 impacts per point, 99.25 % dented (without the factor: λ₁₀₀ = ln 50 = 3.91, 98 % dented). With helical stripes the base time is longer than on an even treatment. The readout “Coverage (NF L 06-832)” is the mean dented area up to the base time tb (98 %), goes from 98 % to 100 % between tb and 1.25·tb, and is 100·t / (1.25·tb) above. A fraction of a pass stands for one pass with the same dose at a proportionally slower traverse speed (which would also make the helix finer).
  11. Back-and-forth passesA return pass draws a helix of the opposite hand. The two families of stripes cross, λ = λ₊(ζ₊) + λ₋(ζ₋), and the mean coverage factorises: τ̄ = 1 − mean(e^(−λ₊))·mean(e^(−λ₋)). Passes in the same direction stack their helices, shifted by a phase that depends on the rotation angle at the start of each pass.

Educational model: the maps show the expected number of impacts per point (mean field); random impacts make the local coverage τ = 1 − e−λ. Constant rotation and traverse speeds, the jet keeps firing beyond the ends of the cylinder (overtravel), parallel jet aimed at the axis, dent size independent of the incidence angle.
